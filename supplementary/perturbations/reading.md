# Reading the perturbation study

> Written by hand after the run. The tables are in [README.md](README.md), generated by
> `edufidelity perturbations`. Three synthetic pages of a fictitious course: **these are
> not results of the study**, and nothing here says how the measures behave on
> LLM-generated pages. It says how they respond to defects introduced on purpose.

## What each measure saw

**SSIM is dominated by the background.** A blank page scores 0.89 to 0.93 against its
reference. That is more than the same page zoomed to 115% (0.80 to 0.87), with every
element still there. On the lesson page, removing one essential paragraph (0.893) scores
the same as erasing the whole page (0.893). SSIM is computed in grayscale, so it barely
sees color: a 180° hue rotation keeps it at 0.999.

**UIClip responds to the look of the page, not to its content.** The range is narrow: from
0.82 (blank) to 1.0. A 180° hue rotation (0.975 to 0.986) costs more than removing an
essential element (0.989 to 0.999). This fits what the model was trained for, which is
judging design quality and relevance. It is a reason to keep it as one predictor among
six, and not to read it as content fidelity. The worst-band diagnostic is never higher
than the page score, but it adds little here: these pages are 1280 x 800, so each one is
seen through only two windows.

**Element Preservation** falls exactly when elements disappear (remove, truncate, blank)
and stays at 1.0 under shift, recolor, font and zoom.

**Layout Fidelity** falls under every displacement and is very brittle: a 115% zoom moves
every box, and the mean IoU drops to 0.14. This is open question Q1 of the engineering plan,
now with a measured example.

**Salience-conditioned preservation** is the only evidence that separates removing an
essential element from removing a decorative one: the first lowers *Essential* (0.81 on
average) and leaves *Decorative* at 1.0; the second does the opposite. SSIM and UIClip
rank the two defects close to each other.

## The 9 checks that failed, and why

All 9 come from `truncate-half`, and **all are mistakes in the expectations, not in the
measures.** The expectations were written before the run and are left as they were.

- **Layout Fidelity did not fall (3 pages).** It averages IoU over *matched* elements only.
  Truncating the bottom half removes elements without moving the ones that remain, so
  the mean stays at 1.0. Missing elements are counted by Element Preservation and the
  salience measures, not by this one. The expectation was wrong about the definition.
- **Salience measures fell where no drop was expected (6 cases).** Truncation removes
  elements of every salience class, so these measures *should* fall. The expectation
  was incomplete.

## Graded perturbations that were not monotonic

Three series improve slightly as the defect gets stronger. All differences are at the
third decimal place:

| Page | Series | Values (identity, then increasing intensity) |
|---|---|---|
| lesson | shift, UIClip | 1.000, 0.998, 0.992, 0.994 |
| quiz | shift, SSIM | 1.000, 0.979, 0.971, 0.972 |
| overview | recolor, SSIM | 1.000, 0.9995, 0.9996 |

A 128 px shift can land the element on blank background and overlap less content than a
32 px shift does. Neither measure promises monotonic behavior. The study records it.

## Consequences for the method (for the authors to decide)

1. SSIM should not be read alone: on these pages it cannot tell a missing paragraph from
   a missing page.
2. UIClip adds a dimension that the others lack (color and overall look), and little
   about content. That supports keeping it, as planned, as a separate predictor whose
   weight comes from calibration.
3. Layout Fidelity needs a decision on Q1 before the study: as defined, a uniform zoom
   counts as a near-total loss of layout.
4. Long pages: the UIClip checkpoint is applied as its authors apply it (mean over sliding
   224 px windows), which answers the concern about squeezing a full page into 224 px.
   A study with long pages is still missing; these pages are short.
